# Where the big dimples come from: obstacles, water depth, and baselines

Notes gathered 2026-09-27 for the question: are the noticeable Rhine dimples shed by bed prominences (boulders, rock outcrops), and did low water make them more common? DOIs checked on Crossref by the research agents; the Nidelva caption was checked directly in the arXiv text.

## Obstacles shed surface-reaching vortices
- **Aarnes, Babiker, Xuan, Shen & Ellingsen (2025)**, *J. Fluid Mech.* 1007:A38, doi:10.1017/jfm.2025.72.
  - Fig. 1 shows the Nidelva in Trondheim. Caption: "The largest dimples (green) are von Kármán vortices shed from a nearby bridge pillar."
  - The photo is illustrative only; the paper gives no counts.
- **Lloyd & Stansby (1997)**, *J. Hydraul. Eng.* 123(12):1068, doi:10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9429(1997)123:12(1068).
  - Submerged conical islands shed vortices only when the water above the apex is shallow.
  - Shedding weakens and stops as the water deepens.
- **Shamloo, Rajaratnam & Katopodis (2001)**, *J. Hydraul. Res.* 39(4):351, doi:10.1080/00221680109499840.
  - Hemispheres in open channels fall into regimes by depth/height, as summarized by Golpira et al. 2023, doi:10.1016/j.ecoleng.2023.106997:
    - depth/height > 4: no effect at the surface;
    - 1.3–4: surface waves;
    - 1–1.3: mixing over the full depth;
    - below 1 (emergent): a Kármán street.
- **Hajimirzaie & Buchholz (2013)**, *Exp. Fluids* 54:1616, doi:10.1007/s00348-013-1616-1.
  - At depth/height 3.9, smooth semi-ellipsoids shed clearly (St ≈ 0.18), while sharp-edged flat-topped cylinders shed "much weaker".
  - At depth/height 1, all shapes shed alternating vortices (St 0.24–0.36).
- **Nilsson et al. (2025)**, *Water* 17:217, doi:10.3390/w17020217. For simplified stones, the hemisphere Strouhal number rises from 0.19 to 0.49 as depth/radius falls from 2 to 1.5.
- **Acarlar & Smith (1987)**, *J. Fluid Mech.* 175:1, doi:10.1017/S0022112087000272.
  - A hemisphere in a laminar boundary layer sheds hairpins very regularly.
  - The shedding becomes irregular above a height-based Reynolds number of about 3400 (via arXiv:1708.06944).
- **Manhart (1998)**, *Theor. Comput. Fluid Dyn.* 12:1, doi:10.1007/s001620050096. In an LES of a hemisphere at Re 1.5×10⁵ in a turbulent boundary layer, alternating shedding is the main wake event.
- **Akilli & Rockwell (2002)**, *Phys. Fluids* 14:2957, doi:10.1063/1.1483307. Shallow Kármán vortices pump fluid up their centres and deform the surface.
- **Chen & Jirka (1995)**, *Fluid Dyn. Res.* 16:11, doi:10.1016/0169-5983(95)00053-G. For emergent bodies, a vortex street needs the shallow-wake parameter S = c_f·D/H < ~0.2.
- **Sukhodolov & Sukhodolova (2014)**, doi:10.1007/s10652-013-9324-1. Caveat: behind a real emergent bar, the predicted street did not form.
- **Tritico & Hotchkiss (2005)**, *J. Hydraul. Eng.* 131(8):635, doi:10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9429(2005)131:8(635). Wakes of emergent boulders were measured in gravel-bed rivers.
- **No study found** that films or measures dimples behind a fully submerged boulder in a real river, or how they change with water level.

## Boils, relative roughness, and water depth
- **Best (2005)**, *JGR Earth Surf.* 110:F04S02, doi:10.1029/2004JF000218. The surface signature over dunes is stronger at higher dune height/depth (a cited LES shows strong interaction at 0.31 and weak at 0.15).
- **Babakaiff & Hickin (1996)**, Squamish estuary. The boil period levels off at its shortest once dune height/depth exceeds about 0.2. Read via Best 2005, not the original.
- **Cooper, Tait & Horoshenkov (2006)**, *ESPL* 31:1839, doi:10.1002/esp.1447. In a flume, shallower and rougher flow gave many small boils; deeper and smoother flow gave fewer, larger ones.
- **Muraro, Dolcetti, Nichols, Tait & Horoshenkov (2021)**, *J. Hydraul. Res.* 59(1):1, doi:10.1080/00221686.2020.1870007.
  - The review calls the role of relative submergence "unclear, and results by different authors are contradicting".
  - Includes dimple photos from the Donaukanal in Vienna.
- **Branch et al. (2021)**, *GRL* 48:e2020GL092326, doi:10.1029/2020GL092326. On the Snohomish River, a rougher bed gives stronger surface turbulence, seen in infrared video.
- **Matthes (1947)**, doi:10.1029/TR028i002p00255; **Kostaschuk & Church (1993)**, doi:10.1016/0037-0738(93)90073-E; **Roy et al. (2004)**, *J. Fluid Mech.* 500:1, doi:10.1017/S0022112003006396.
- **No published baseline** for dimple density on any river, the Rhine included. No field study of dimple counts against depth or discharge.

## The Rhine
- No published observations of boils or dimples on any stretch.
- Near Mainz, dunes are under 1 m high in about 4 m of water (Carling et al. 2000, *Sedimentology* 47:227, doi:10.1046/j.1365-3091.2000.00290.x).
- **High Rhine** (Masterplan Geschiebereaktivierung Hochrhein, 2013):
  - The river leaves Lake Constance with essentially no sediment load and runs over a gravel bed, in places cut down to bedrock. There is no sand, so no dunes.
  - Near-natural mean velocity is about 1–1.3 m/s.
  - From Diessenhofen to Schaffhausen it is backed up by the power plant.
- FOEN gauge 2288 at Neuhausen: mean discharge 365 m³/s.
- Local news reports that passenger boats stopped running between Stein am Rhein and Diessenhofen from 27 June 2026 because of low water. This is secondhand and not checked against gauge data.

## How to tell boulder-shed dimples from ordinary bed-turbulence dimples
Watch from the bank or an anchored boat.

| | Boulder-shed | Ordinary bed turbulence |
|---|---|---|
| Where they start | a fixed spot relative to the bank | anywhere across the width, often at the edges of boils |
| Timing | periodic: f ≈ St·U/D, about one every 2–5 s for D ≈ 1 m at U ≈ 1 m/s | no rhythm |
| Pattern | a narrow band, spaced about 3–5 D; alternating spins in two staggered rows (shallow), or counter-rotating pairs (deeper) | random spin senses, no order |
| Over the source | often a bulge or standing wave | nothing fixed |
| Lower water | expected to get stronger | no clear prediction |
